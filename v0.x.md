# v0.x — Plan (version number not yet assigned)

**Naming note**: this is deliberately *not* called `v0.4.md`. It's for some upcoming 0.x release
that comes after v0.3 (which already has its own `v0.3.md`) and after v0.2 (already shipped/in
bugfix maintenance) — but exactly which 0.x number it'll end up as hasn't been decided. Once that's
settled, rename this file to match (e.g. `v0.4.md`) and update `Versionsplan.md`'s heading to match.

Companion to `v0.3.md` (the prior minor version's detailed plan) and `Mysc/Versionsplan.md` (the
high-level roadmap, now updated: this release sits between v0.3 and v1.0 specifically to land the
faction system and a reactive world **before** v1.0, since v1.0's scope requires the full story —
including the Luria/Tyriova war — to already be playable). Started 2026-09-13, very early stage:
this is a placeholder capturing what's been decided so far, not yet a build-ready spec the way
v0.3's items are.

---

## Why this is its own (not yet numbered) version, not part of v0.3 or folded into v1.0

- **Not v0.3**: v0.3 is already large (versioning scheme, equipment tooltips, job-page crafting,
  combat item use, PvP rooms, more dungeons, the quest system rework). Both items below are
  individually comparable in scope to the biggest things already in v0.3 — adding them there would
  roughly double that release.
- **Not v1.0 or later**: v1.0 needs the full story playable already, and per the person planning
  this, the Luria/Tyriova war (Lurian Kingdom defending, Tyriovan Empire attacking, both present as
  playable factions *within* Luria's story — not a separate travelable Tyriova homeland, which
  stays out of scope until a Post-v1.0 update) is core to that story. So the faction system can't be
  pushed past v1.0 — it has to land in its own version in between.

## Faction system — playable Lurian Kingdom vs. Tyriovan Empire, war-state PvP

### Behavior (decided so far)

- Players belong to one of two factions: the Lurian Kingdom (defending) or the Tyriovan Empire
  (attacking) — both present and playable within Luria itself, matching the SP and MP storylines'
  war. This is not a second travelable region; the Tyriovan Empire's presence in v1.0-era content is
  as an invading faction inside Luria.
- The two factions are at war. Players from factions at war with each other can attack one another —
  this extends PvP **beyond** v0.3's dedicated-rooms-only model: once this lands, faction-vs-faction
  combat is allowed everywhere, not just inside a designated PvP room. v0.3's PvP room system and
  its underlying encounter engine are the direct foundation this builds on/extends, not a competing
  system.

### Open questions — this needs a real design pass before it's buildable

- Is a player's faction chosen at character creation and permanent, or can it change later (and if
  so, how)?
- How does faction membership interact with existing Luria content — do both factions play through
  the same starting content with faction-specific war elements layered on top, or does each faction
  get its own distinct starting experience?
- Is the Luria/Tyriova war always "hot" (open PvP always active once this ships), or does it
  escalate/de-escalate over time — and if the latter, that's presumably one of the things the
  reactive world system (below) would drive.
- Does v0.3's room-based PvP stay as its own separate, faction-agnostic "duel anywhere, anyone"
  system once this ships, or does faction war PvP fully absorb/replace it?

## Reactive world — the world responds to collective player behavior, tied to factions

### Behavior (decided so far)

- Rooms, monster populations, cities, and other world state should react to what players
  collectively do: which quests get completed, where players tend to gather, which jobs players
  choose and actively work. This should visibly shape how the world develops over time — new cities
  rising, others declining, monster spawn locations shifting, and more beyond just those examples.
- This is explicitly bidirectional with the faction system: the war's state should influence how the
  world develops, and the world's development should in turn influence the war/factions — not a
  one-way feed from factions into the world.

### Reuse from v0.3 — this is the natural foundation, not a coincidence

- The quest system rework already being planned for v0.3 introduces exactly the kind of world-state
  telemetry this needs: per-NPC item stock/thresholds, per-room monster population counters, and a
  new background tick service to drive them. A reactive world system would consume that same data
  (and the tick infrastructure) rather than needing its own from scratch.

### Open questions — this is greenfield and needs substantial design work

- What's the actual "unit" of world reaction — a per-city or per-region prosperity/influence score,
  something else? What concretely changes when it moves (new NPCs, new shops, a room's tier, visual
  changes, available quests)?
- What does "a city rises, another falls" concretely mean mechanically — is this cosmetic/flavor
  (renamed status, different dialogue), or does it materially change available content (shops,
  quests, jobs) in that location?
- How is "where players gather" measured — presence/time-spent, or something else?
- How exactly does the faction war feed into this, and how does this feed back into the war (e.g.
  does a city's prosperity affect which faction can contest it, does the war's front line shift based
  on player activity)?

## Class/magic system — bring it in line with the story's magic lore

**Confirmed via code + lore comparison — real design work, not a quick reflavor, but not a ground-up
rebuild either.**

### The gap, concretely

`Myria.Story`'s Magic System doc defines three sharply distinct kinds of magic:
- **Outer magic** — an uncontrollable planetary force (weather, climate, plant growth). No creature
  can wield it; it's environmental lore, not a player-usable mechanic. (See its own section below —
  this connects to the reactive-world idea above.)
- **Inner magic** — personal, soul-based, inborn. Hard rule: **one being can only ever have one
  element** (fire, water, ice, earth, air, plant, or arcane) — a water mage literally cannot touch
  ice, and it's fueled by a personal mana pool that depletes with use. Tied to race as a tendency,
  not a fixed class. **Amato (humans) have none at all.**
- **Rune magic** — a degraded divine script (Tsāmwas, dragon-given), independent of inner magic, so
  humans can use it despite having no inner magic. Not bound to one element — a single rune-caster
  can reach multiple elements through different words — but slow, precision-dependent, and always
  temporary; canonically weak by the story's era.

Today's classes (`ElementalMage`, `ArcanMage`, `Druid`, `Cleric`, `SoulsKnight`, `RunicMage`,
`Data/common/classes.json`) are mechanically just "INT/SPR-scaling magic with different flavor
text" — nothing enforces the single-element rule (a live `ElementalMage` can cast fire, ice, *and*
lightning simultaneously today, which the lore explicitly forbids as lethal), and `Cleric`/`Druid`/
`SoulsKnight` have no counterpart at all in the lore's three-kind taxonomy — homebrew categories
that need their own justification or folding into something else.

Two encouraging findings, though — this isn't starting from zero:
- `Amato`/humans are already barred from every inner-magic class but allowed `RunicMage`
  (`Data/common/races.json`'s `forbiddenClasses`) — that's the lore's human/rune-magic rule already
  correctly encoded.
- `RunicMage`'s own data model (word-based runes, multi-element access per caster) is already the
  *most* lore-accurate of all six classes — it's only disabled today because of the unrelated
  hand-drawing recognition bug (see `TODO`/earlier notes on why RunicMage was cut), not because its
  design contradicts the lore. Fixing that recognition bug and doing this lore-alignment work are
  two separate problems that happen to share a class.

### Why this doesn't fit v0.3

Beyond sheer size, the real cost driver is **existing character migration**: enforcing a
single-element lock on `ElementalMage` (etc.) means deciding what happens to already-existing beta
characters who currently have mixed-element skill loadouts — not just new-character-creation logic.
That's a meaningfully different risk profile than v0.3's other items, and v0.3 is already large and
mid-beta-testing.

### Open questions — needs a real design pass later

- What happens to `Cleric`/`Druid`/`SoulsKnight` — reworked into lore-justified variants (e.g. is
  "divine" magic actually a real thing in the lore worth retconning in, or should these fold into
  rune-magic or inner-magic variants), or removed/replaced entirely?
- How does an existing character with mixed-element skills get migrated once single-element-lock is
  enforced — forced to pick one, refunded skill points, grandfathered as an exception, something
  else?
- Does `RunicMage`'s recognition-bug fix land as part of this work, or stay a separate fix that can
  happen independently (the two are decoupled per the finding above, so no hard dependency either
  way)?

## Outer magic — an environmental world-force system (very early, "at some point" per the request)

The lore's outer magic (an uncontrollable planetary force driving weather/climate/plant growth) has
no player-facing mechanic today and isn't urgent — noted here mainly so it isn't lost, and because
it naturally connects to the **reactive world** idea above: a weather/climate/environmental layer
driven by (or influencing) the same world-state simulation, rather than being a wholly separate
system. No design has happened on this yet; revisit once reactive world itself is further along.
